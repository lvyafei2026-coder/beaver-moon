The Beaver Moon: November's Full Moon

The Beaver Moon is the full moon that falls in November, and it has carried that name in North American almanacs and folklore for generations. It is an ordinary full moon in astronomical terms — what makes it notable is the season it marks, the stories attached to its name, and the fact that it rises early in the evening and stays up all night. Here is what the name means, where it comes from, and how to watch it.

What the Beaver Moon Actually Is

The Beaver Moon is the traditional name for the full moon of November. It belongs to a set of monthly moon names that circulated widely through almanacs and were rooted in the seasonal calendars of Indigenous peoples in what is now the northeastern United States and eastern Canada. The name does not describe anything about the Moon itself; it marks a point in the year, in the same way that harvest or frost names do.

A full moon is simply the phase when the Moon is opposite the Sun as seen from Earth, so its Earth-facing side is fully lit. That happens once every lunar cycle, roughly every 29.5 days, which is why most calendar months contain exactly one. November's is the one traditionally called the Beaver Moon, though you will sometimes see it listed under other regional names.

Where the Name Comes From

Two explanations for the name show up again and again in almanac write-ups, and they are not mutually exclusive. One is that November is when beavers are busy repairing dams and stocking food before ponds freeze over, so the month's moon was associated with that activity. The other is that it marked the season when beavers were trapped, since their winter fur was at its thickest.

Written sources rarely agree on which explanation is primary, and that is worth knowing before you repeat one as fact. Moon names were passed down orally across many different nations, and different communities used different names for the same month — Frost Moon, Mourning Moon, and others appear in the same lists. Treat any single origin story as one version among several.

When to Look for It

The full moon happens at one precise moment, but to the eye it looks full for roughly a night on either side, so you have some slack. In November, the Moon rises close to sunset for viewers in the northern hemisphere and stays above the horizon through the night, setting around sunrise. That makes it one of the easier full moons to see without rearranging your evening.

The exact date shifts from year to year because the lunar cycle does not line up neatly with the calendar, and the date can also differ by a day between time zones. If you need the precise date and moment for your location, use an astronomical calendar or almanac set to your own time zone rather than a list written for another country.

Is the Beaver Moon a Supermoon?

Sometimes, but not reliably. A supermoon is a full moon that occurs while the Moon is near perigee — the closest point in its elliptical orbit around Earth. Because the orbit and the phase cycle drift against each other, being a November full moon does not guarantee being near perigee. Some years the Beaver Moon is a close, bright one; other years it is not.

Even in a close year, the difference is modest. The Moon's distance varies by roughly a tenth of its average distance, which changes its apparent width by a small percentage and its brightness somewhat more — a difference that is essentially impossible to judge by eye without photographs taken months apart. A supermoon is a real, measurable event, but it is not a dramatically different Moon in the sky.

Watching and Photographing It

Pick a spot with a clear view toward the east and southeast, where the Moon comes up. Moonrise is the most rewarding part of the night: near the horizon the Moon looks larger and often takes on an orange or yellow tint, both effects of looking through more of the atmosphere and of how we perceive objects near the horizon. You need no equipment, though binoculars will show craters along the terminator edge.

For photographs, a tripod and a longer lens do most of the work. Expose for the Moon itself rather than the landscape — it is lit by direct sunlight and is far brighter than it appears — and shoot at moonrise if you want the Moon sharing a frame with buildings or trees. Check the weather forecast and the local moonrise time before heading out, since cloud cover is the one thing that reliably spoils the view.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Beaver Moon?

It is the traditional name for the full moon that occurs in November, used in North American almanacs and folklore. It is a normal full moon, not a distinct astronomical object.

When is the Beaver Moon in 2026?

It falls in November, as it does every year. The precise date and moment depend on your time zone, so check a local astronomical calendar for the exact time where you are.

Why is it called the Beaver Moon?

The usual explanations are that November is when beavers prepare their dams for winter, or that it was the season for trapping beavers for winter fur. Sources differ on which is primary.

Is the Beaver Moon always a supermoon?

No. It depends on whether the full moon happens to occur near perigee that year. Some Novembers it does; most years the Moon is farther away.

Do I need a telescope to see it?

No. A full moon is bright enough to see easily with the naked eye, and binoculars are enough to make out craters and maria along its edge.
